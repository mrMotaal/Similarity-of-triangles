App 1 • Page 55

Ancient Egyptian Shadow Technique

Ancient Egyptians were the first to measure tree heights using stick shadows. A 2m vertical stick casts a 1.2m shadow. What is the height of a palm tree casting a 3.6m shadow?

• Sun angle is constant $\implies$ Similar right triangles (AA).

• $\frac{\text{Tree Height}}{\text{Stick Height}} = \frac{\text{Tree Shadow}}{\text{Stick Shadow}}$

• $\frac{H}{2} = \frac{3.6}{1.2} = 3 \implies \mathbf{H = 6\text{ meters}}$

App 2 • Page 58

School Flagpole Measurement

A student 1.6 m tall casts a 1.5 m shadow. At the same moment, the school flagpole casts a 2.4 m shadow. What is the height of the flagpole?

• $\Delta NML \sim \Delta ABC$ (by AA similarity)

• $\frac{ML}{BC} = \frac{NM}{AB} \implies \frac{ML}{1.6} = \frac{2.4}{1.5}$

• $ML = \frac{2.4 \times 1.6}{1.5} = \mathbf{2.56\text{ meters}}$

App 3 • Page 61

Grand Museum Ramses II Statue

A visitor 1.9m tall places a mirror on the floor 12m from the statue and steps back 2m to view the crown in the mirror where angle of incidence = angle of reflection.

• Since $m(\angle 1) = m(\angle 2)$ and both ground angles are $90^\circ \implies$ AA similarity.

• $\frac{\text{Statue Height}}{1.9} = \frac{12}{2} = 6$

• $\text{Statue Height} = 6 \times 1.9 = \mathbf{11.4\text{ meters}}$

Core Mathematical Foundation & Governing Laws

Two triangles are similar ($\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$) if and only if corresponding angles are congruent and corresponding sides are proportional.

Case 1: Angle-Angle (AA)
If $m(\angle A)=m(\angle D)$ & $m(\angle B)=m(\angle E)$

$\implies \Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$. The third angle is automatically congruent.

Case 2: Three Sides (SSS)
$$\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF} = k$$

Where $k$ is the constant similarity ratio (scale factor).

Case 3: Side-Angle-Side (SAS)
$\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{AC}{DF}$ & $m(\angle A) = m(\angle D)$

The congruent angle must be strictly INCLUDED between proportional sides.

Euclid's Right Triangle
In rt $\Delta$ with altitude $AD \perp BC$:

$AD^2 = BD \times DC$, $AB^2 = BD \times BC$, $AC^2 = CD \times CB$.

Case 1 • Strategy Guide

Angle-Angle Similarity Postulate (AA)

Always search for: 1. Parallel lines producing Alternate Interior (Z-shape) or Corresponding (F-shape) angles. 2. Shared/Common angles. 3. Vertically opposite angles (X-shape).

Example 1.1 • Page 56
Intersecting Chords with Parallel Lines

In the figure: $\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{DC}$, $\overline{AC} \cap \overline{BD} = \{E\}$, $AB = 6$ cm, $DC = 4$ cm, $AE = 7.5$ cm, and $BD = 7$ cm. Prove that $\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE$, then find the lengths of $BE, DE$, and $CE$.

1. Since $\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD} \implies m(\angle A) = m(\angle C)$ (alternate angles).

2. $m(\angle B) = m(\angle D)$ (alternate angles) $\implies \mathbf{\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE}$ (AA).

3. $\frac{AB}{CD} = \frac{BE}{DE} = \frac{AE}{CE} \implies \frac{6}{4} = \frac{7 - DE}{DE} = \frac{7.5}{CE}$

4. $6 \cdot DE = 4(7 - DE) = 28 - 4 \cdot DE \implies 10 \cdot DE = 28 \implies \mathbf{DE = 2.8\text{ cm}}$

5. $\mathbf{BE = 7 - 2.8 = 4.2\text{ cm}}$

6. $CE = \frac{4 \times 7.5}{6} = \mathbf{5\text{ cm}}$

Non-Parallel Triangle with Angle Equality

In the figure: $D \in \overline{AC}$, $E \in \overline{AB}$, $m(\angle ADE) = m(\angle B)$.

4. From similarity ratios: $\frac{AB}{AD} = \frac{AC}{AE} \implies \mathbf{AB \times AE = AC \times AD}$.

In $\Delta ABC$: $D \in \overline{AB}$, $E \in \overline{AC}$, $\overline{DE} \parallel \overline{BC}$. If $AD = 4.5$ cm, $DB = 3.5$ cm, $BC = 6$ cm, $DE = 3$ cm. Prove that $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta ADE$ and find the length of $AE$ and $EC$.

Solution Workspace

• $\frac{AC}{AE} = 2 \implies AE = 4.5\text{ cm}$.

Case 2 • Strategy Guide

Three Proportional Sides (SSS)

Order the 3 side lengths in each triangle ascendingly from smallest to largest. If all three simplify to the exact same ratio $k$, similarity is confirmed!

Example 2.1 • Page 56
Proving SSS with Scale Factor Ratio

In $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta ADE$: $AB = 8$ cm, $AD = 4$ cm, $BC = 6$ cm, $DE = 3$ cm, $AC = 9$ cm, $AE = 4.5$ cm. Prove that $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta ADE$ stating the scale factor.

1. Check side ratios: $\frac{AB}{AD} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$.

2. $\frac{BC}{DE} = \frac{6}{3} = 2$.

3. $\frac{AC}{AE} = \frac{9}{4.5} = 2$.

4. Since $\frac{AB}{AD} = \frac{BC}{DE} = \frac{AC}{AE} = 2 \implies \mathbf{\Delta ABC \sim \Delta ADE}$ (SSS).

5. Scale factor of $\Delta ABC$ to $\Delta ADE$ is $\mathbf{2 : 1}$.

Case 3 • Strategy Guide

Side-Angle-Side (SAS) Postulate

Verify two conditions: 1. Exactly two pairs of sides are proportional. 2. The angle strictly between those two sides is congruent.

Example 3.1 • Page 57
Vertical Angles with SAS Proportions

In the figure: $m(\angle ACB) = m(\angle DCE)$, $AC = 9$ cm, $DC = 6$ cm, $BC = 12$ cm, $EC = 8$ cm, and $DE = 10$ cm. Prove that $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEC$, then find the length of $AB$.

1. Ratio 1: $\frac{AC}{DC} = \frac{9}{6} = \frac{3}{2}$.

2. Ratio 2: $\frac{BC}{EC} = \frac{12}{8} = \frac{3}{2}$.

3. Included angle: $m(\angle ACB) = m(\angle DCE)$.

4. $\therefore \mathbf{\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEC}$ by SAS.

5. From similarity: $\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{3}{2} \implies \frac{AB}{10} = \frac{3}{2} \implies \mathbf{AB = 15\text{ cm}}$.

Example 3.2 • Page 60 Q1
Side Proportion with Shared Angle

In $\Delta ABC$: $AE = 6$ cm, $EB = 9$ cm, $AD = 5$ cm, $DC = 13$ cm, $ED = 4$ cm. Prove that $\Delta ADE \sim \Delta ABC$ and find the length of $BC$.

1. $AB = 6 + 9 = 15$ cm, $AC = 5 + 13 = 18$ cm.

2. Ratios: $\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{5}{15} = \frac{1}{3}$, $\frac{AE}{AC} = \frac{6}{18} = \frac{1}{3}$.

3. $\angle A$ is common $\implies \mathbf{\Delta ADE \sim \Delta ABC}$ (SAS).

4. $\frac{ED}{BC} = \frac{1}{3} \implies \frac{4}{BC} = \frac{1}{3} \implies \mathbf{BC = 12\text{ cm}}$.

Try It Yourself 3 (Self Assessment 3 • Page 57):

In the figure: $\overline{AC} \cap \overline{DB} = \{E\}$, $AE = 15$ cm, $EB = 30$ cm, $CE = 9$ cm, $ED = 18$ cm, $AB = 25$ cm. Prove that $\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE$, then find the length of $DC$.

Solution Workspace

• $\frac{CE}{AE} = \frac{9}{15} = \frac{3}{5}$, $\frac{ED}{EB} = \frac{18}{30} = \frac{3}{5}$.

• $m(\angle CED) = m(\angle AEB)$ (vertically opposite) $\implies \Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE$.

• $\frac{DC}{AB} = \frac{3}{5} \implies DC = \frac{3 \times 25}{5} = \mathbf{15\text{ cm}}$.

Special Case • Euclid's Theorems

Altitude to Hypotenuse in Right Triangles

If $m(\angle A) = 90^\circ$ and $\overline{AD} \perp \overline{BC}$, then: $\Delta DBA \sim \Delta DAC \sim \Delta ABC$.
Master formulas: 1. $AD^2 = BD \cdot DC$   2. $AB^2 = BD \cdot BC$   3. $AC^2 = CD \cdot CB$   4. $AD = \frac{AB \cdot AC}{BC}$.

Example 4.1 • Page 58
Proof of Altitude Theorem: $(AD)^2 = BD \times DC$

$\Delta ABC$ is right-angled at $A$ and $\overline{AD} \perp \overline{BC}$. Prove that $\Delta ADB \sim \Delta CDA$, and deduce that $(AD)^2 = BD \times DC$.

1. In $\Delta ADB$ and $\Delta CDA$: $m(\angle ADB) = m(\angle CDA) = 90^\circ$.

2. $m(\angle DAB) = 90^\circ - m(\angle B) = m(\angle C)$.

3. $\therefore \mathbf{\Delta ADB \sim \Delta CDA}$ (AA).

4. $\frac{AD}{CD} = \frac{DB}{DA} \implies \mathbf{(AD)^2 = BD \times DC}$.

Example 4.2 • Page 61 Q12
Numerical Calculation using Euclid: Finding $DC$

In rt $\Delta ABC$ at $B$, $\overline{BD} \perp \overline{AC}$. If $AD = 6.4$ cm, $BD = 4.8$ cm, find the length of $DC$.

1. By Euclid's theorem: $(BD)^2 = AD \times DC$.

2. $(4.8)^2 = 6.4 \times DC \implies 23.04 = 6.4 \times DC$.

3. $\mathbf{DC = \frac{23.04}{6.4} = 3.6\text{ cm}}$.

In the figure: $m(\angle ABC) = 90^\circ$, $\overline{BD} \perp \overline{AC}$, $AB = 3$ cm, $BC = 4$ cm. Find the lengths of $AC, AD$, and $DC$.
